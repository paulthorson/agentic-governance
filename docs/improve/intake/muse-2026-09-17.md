## Improvements shipped

**Post-ALLOW micro-changes need their own focused re-review**

- A workstream closed after two post-ALLOW micro-changes (a file-lock guard and PID-collision-proof spill filenames) — each delta got its own focused blind re-review before close. "Tiny" changes to locking and filename uniqueness are exactly where races hide. Now a standing rule in the runtime: any code touched after an ALLOW gets a focused blind review of the delta before the workstream closes.
- Related structural finding: a round-3 blind reviewer proposed the actual structural fix (unifying CLI/MCP store resolution through one environment variable with explicit precedence) that the builders had missed across two full rounds. Blind reviewers earn their keep on architecture, not just on bugs.

**Durability code needs adversarial thread tests, not just unit tests**

- A versioned trust-store's crash-recovery redesign passed unit tests, but a 60-iteration concurrency stress test caught the first cut's reader-visible race (15 of 60 runs silently served a stale generation); the redesign scored 0 of 60. Now a runtime rule: concurrency-sensitive durability code ships with an adversarial thread test.
- Lock/recovery docstrings are load-bearing defects when wrong: one falsely claimed a lock serialized recoveries (atomicity actually came from hard-link rename plus loser re-verification), another overclaimed stale-temp cleanup. A maintainer trusting either sentence could delete the real mechanism and reintroduce the race. Honest caveats (file locks coordinate cooperating processes only; single-generation backups) are a feature, not a weakness.

**Reviews gate commits, never follow them**

- Three built workstreams were committed before their required QA reviews ran — a process error (builders never verify their own work). The subsequent blind QA KICK_BACKs forced rework whose cost dwarfed the initial build. The framework's rule exists to prevent exactly this failure; the miss is the evidence. Commit nothing ahead of its review.
- Rework must sweep the flaw class across all call sites, not just the flagged instance: reviewers kept finding the same flaw one level deeper at a sibling call site (sanitized file-prefs, unsanitized explicit-prefs; guarded guided-update, unguarded CLI record path). The rework instruction is now "fix the class everywhere," verified by the reviewer probing adjacent call sites.

**The "one level deeper" probe pattern (AST contract generator, 6 review rounds)**

- An AST-based contract generator reached technical ALLOW only at round 24 after six rounds of blind QA, each round's reviewer independently verifying prior fix claims with their own harness, then probing one level deeper: nested subscript, dynamic fetches, cross-surface calls, aliased cross-surface — every fix exposed an adjacent silent-drop shape. The gate's success criterion — every unmodeled construct is recorded or refused, never silently dropped — is the right bar, but achieving it requires reviewers who probe beyond the claimed fix. The builder cannot see their own blind spots.
- The stream stays parked: it holds a technical ALLOW under an uncleared legal veto, so nothing ships. A KICK_BACK nobody may clear except the human is the loop holding work, not the loop failing.

**Review machinery needs the same adversarial posture as the code**

- The keyword veto scanner fired KICK_BACK records on three test/doc comments ("no data loss", "no API key", "zero downtime") — pure false positives. The records stay in the append-only log; triage ALLOW records explaining each were appended, not edited. Unresolved governance question: the standing rules say only the human clears a veto; these machine-generated KICK_BACKs have no human overturn, and they must not be silently treated as deleted.
- Two verdict-log defects found in one day: (1) two records share one ticket (`stack-contracts-r4`) with opposite verdicts (ALLOW, then KICK_BACK) — append-only ordering disambiguates, but the naming collision is a hazard; verdict records need timestamps and unique record IDs (echoes the 2026-09-13 lesson that verdicts can't prove what text they judged without timestamps). (2) The review-start output truncates prompts at a fixed stdout size, silently corrupting saved start records — fixed in the runtime (no truncation; stdout is a machine contract) and recorded rather than silently patched.
- A bundle generator ran under the wrong interpreter and reported a passing-but-empty test count — fixed to the project interpreter with a count-greater-than-zero sanity check, all bundles regenerated with real counts. Generators misreport; sanity checks on the harness output are part of the harness.

**Evidence over assumption in legal review**

- The compliance reviewer fetched the third party's current terms of service rather than asserting a violation from memory — that is what makes a legal review worth anything. One module's default-on automated web research was the one automated third-party access never previously compliance-reviewed; it is parked for the human's explicit opt-in-or-accept decision. New runtime convention: any automated third-party network access defaults to explicit opt-in (the notification module's opt-in pattern became the internal standard), never default-on.
